# Getting Started

Welcome to UCA CyRange. This is a hands-on cyber range built around industrial control systems — the kind of infrastructure that runs power grids, water treatment plants, factory floors, and modern vehicles. If you've never touched an ICS environment before, that's fine. The platform is designed to walk you in gradually.

---

## What you'll find here

UCA CyRange is organized around four learning paths: **Risk Management**, **Offensive**, **Defensive**, and **Mitigation**. Each path contains modules, and each module contains rooms. A room is a self-contained scenario — you get a story, a set of tasks, and real virtual machines to work with. You don't watch videos or read slides. You investigate, explore, and figure things out by doing.

The first room available is **Interrupted OTA Update**, inside the Accidental Risk module of the Risk Management path. It's a good place to start regardless of your background. The scenario is realistic, the evidence is ambiguous, and the point isn't to find the right answer quickly — it's to learn how to think through an incident properly.

---

## Your first session

**1. Log in and go to the Roadmap.**
The roadmap shows all four paths with their modules and rooms. Published rooms have a green indicator. Draft modules are visible but locked — they're coming as the platform grows.

**2. Open the Interrupted OTA Update room.**
Read the story. It sets the scene. Then read the objectives — they tell you exactly what skills this room is testing.

**3. Start the lab.**
Click "Start Lab" on the room detail page. The platform will provision three virtual machines on the Proxmox cluster in the background. This usually takes under a minute. You'll see the status update in real time.

**4. Work through the tasks in order.**
Each task has a written description that tells you what to look at and where. Read it carefully before you touch anything. Task 1 asks you to observe before analyzing — that's intentional.

**5. Submit answers as you go.**
Questions appear at the bottom of each task. Some are multiple choice, some ask you to enter a flag or a specific value you found in a log. Your answers are saved automatically.

---

## The timer

Every lab session has a time limit — 120 minutes for most rooms. The clock starts when you launch the lab, not when you start answering questions. If you need to step away, use the **Pause** button. The timer freezes and the VMs suspend. When you come back and click **Resume**, everything picks up from exactly where you left it.

If the timer runs out, the VMs are destroyed automatically. Your answers and progress are saved, but you'll need to start a new session to access the machines again.

---

## XP and progress

Every question you answer correctly awards XP. Your total is shown in the top bar. Progress through tasks, rooms, and modules is tracked separately — you can see it on the Dashboard. Completing a module unlocks a short quiz that tests the concepts across all its rooms.

---

## A note on difficulty

The rooms are not CTF competitions. There are no trick questions or obscure exploits. The difficulty comes from reading evidence carefully, correlating information across multiple sources, and making a judgment call that's proportional to what you've actually found — not what would make the best story. That's the skill set we're building here.

If you get stuck, hints are available inside each question. Some hints are free, others cost a small XP penalty. Use them without guilt — knowing when to ask for help is part of the process.
